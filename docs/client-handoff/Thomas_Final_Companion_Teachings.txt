THE WOLF

Leadership • Foresight • Protection • Responsibility

Expanded Teaching

The wolf belongs to the pack, but leadership sometimes requires walking ahead.

I think of the wolf as a reflection of something I have come to understand about being a Chief.

A Chief does not stand above the people simply to possess authority.

The Chief carries responsibility for the people.

Sometimes leadership means going ahead, looking at what may be waiting beyond the place where everyone else can see, recognizing danger, and determining whether the people should continue forward, change direction, or come together because their collective strength is needed.

The people are not possessions of the Chief.

They are the reason for the responsibility.

Leadership also depends upon trust.

People follow when they believe the person walking ahead is acting not merely for himself, but with the welfare of the people in mind.

There is humility in that responsibility because leadership is not something a Chief should take for granted.

A Chief does not walk ahead so that others may see the Chief leading.

A Chief walks ahead because someone must look down the road for those who are coming behind.

The Wolf reminds me that sometimes I may have to walk ahead of the pack.

Sometimes I may have to walk alone.

But the purpose of walking ahead is always to remember who is coming behind me.

That is how I understand leadership.

What We Are Asked to Remember

Not power over the people.
 Responsibility to the people.

THE HAWK

Vision • Discernment • Foresight

Expanded Teaching

The Hawk reminds me to look beyond what is immediately before me.

There are times in a journey when we can become so occupied with the ground beneath our feet that we lose sight of where the road may be leading.

The Hawk offers another visual direction.

From above, what appeared separate begins to reveal its relationship to everything around it.

Leadership sometimes requires that wider view.

I must see not only where I am standing, but where those who come behind me may one day need to go.

The Hawk does not give me that responsibility.

Life already has.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Hawk simply reminds me to look far enough ahead to remember it.

THE BEAR

Strength • Protection • Stewardship

Expanded Teaching

The Bear reminds me that strength does not always need to announce itself.

There is a strength that stands quietly because it knows what it is responsible for protecting.

As Chief and Elder, I have come to understand that strength carries responsibility.

It is not given so that we may demonstrate power over others.

It is needed so that when something entrusted to our care requires protection, we are strong enough to stand.

Sometimes protection requires action.

Sometimes it requires patience.

Sometimes it is simply refusing to move away from something that must not be abandoned.

The Bear does not teach me to be strong.

Life has already taught me about strength.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Bear reminds me what strength is for.

THE RAVEN

Memory • Voice • Continuity • Carrying Forward

Expanded Teaching

The Raven reminds me that memory must have a carrier.

A story can live for generations, but only if someone remembers it, speaks it, writes it down, or places it into the hands of someone prepared to carry it farther.

I have come to understand that the voice is capable of carrying much more than music.

The voice can carry memory.

It can carry the names of those who came before us.

It can speak of lives that might otherwise be forgotten.

It can bring into the light something that has remained unheard for too long.

And when that memory is carried faithfully, it does not have to end with the person who first spoke it.

It can become inheritance.

The Raven does not give me my voice.

The journey has already done that.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Raven simply reminds me to remember why I must use it.

THE OWL

Listening • Reflection • Discernment • Truth

Expanded Teaching

The Owl reminds me that not everything worth knowing reveals itself immediately.

There are truths we see in daylight, when everything appears clear before us.

But there are other truths that require stillness.

They ask us to listen longer, look more carefully, and sometimes become quiet enough to recognize what has been there all along.

As an Elder, I have learned that wisdom is not having an answer for everything.

Sometimes wisdom is knowing when not to speak.

Sometimes it is listening until what matters becomes clear.

And sometimes it means having the courage to bring into the light what has remained hidden in darkness.

The Owl does not give me that discernment.

Life, experience, mistakes, loss, survival, and reflection have been teaching it to me for many years.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Owl simply reminds me to remain quiet enough to hear what I am supposed to remember.

THE PANTHER

Patience • Discernment • Presence • Right Action

Expanded Teaching

The Panther reminds me that not every moment requires movement.

There are times when wisdom means waiting, watching, and allowing the situation before us to reveal itself.

Life has taught me that action taken simply because we are impatient to act can sometimes create more difficulty than it resolves.

There is another kind of strength.

The strength to remain still until we understand why we are moving.

That does not mean fear.

It does not mean indecision.

It means learning the difference between merely reacting and acting with purpose.

As a Chief, an Elder, and simply as a human being who has travelled a long road, I have learned that there are moments when I must listen before I speak, observe before I decide, and understand before I act.

And when the time for action arrives, I must be prepared to step forward.

The Panther does not give me patience or discernment.

The journey has been teaching those lessons for a lifetime.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Panther simply reminds me that strength also knows when to wait, and when waiting is finished.

THE DEER

Generations • Stewardship • Continuity • Legacy

Expanded Teaching

The Deer reminds me that the journey does not begin with us, and it does not belong only to us.

We arrive upon a path already carrying something.

We carry family.

We carry memory.

We carry the consequences of what others endured so that we could stand where we stand today.

And eventually we begin to understand that something has also been placed into our care.

What was carried to us must not end with us.

As an Elder, I have become increasingly aware that some of the work I do today is not meant primarily for me.

Some of it belongs to children and grandchildren.

Some belongs to people I may never meet.

Some belongs to a generation that will one day look backward and ask whether those who came before them remembered to leave something behind.

That responsibility changes the way I understand legacy.

Legacy is not merely what people remember about us.

Legacy is what remains available to them because we remembered them.

The Deer does not give me that responsibility.

My family, my ancestry, my life, and the road I have travelled have already taught it to me.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Deer simply reminds me to leave the path visible for those who are still coming.

THE EAGLE

Majesty • Sacred Responsibility • Perspective • Reverence

Expanded Teaching

The Eagle reminds me that there are some things we do not simply look upon. We look upon them with reverence.

There is a majesty about the Eagle that asks us to stop and recognize that not everything in this world exists merely for our use.

I do not wish to reduce the Eagle to decoration or assign meanings to it carelessly.

For me, the Eagle is a reminder that responsibility can also be sacred.

To rise high enough to see farther does not place us above the people.

It allows us to see more of what we have been entrusted to protect.

A Chief must sometimes see beyond the present moment.

An Elder must sometimes remember beyond one lifetime.

And both must understand that what has been entrusted to them was never given merely for possession.

It was given to be carried with responsibility.

The Eagle does not give me that responsibility.

My journey has already been teaching it.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Eagle reminds me of the reverence with which that responsibility must be carried.

THE FOX

Awareness • Cunning • Adaptability • Another Way

Expanded Teaching

The Fox reminds me that not every obstacle must be confronted head-on.

Life sometimes places something across our path that cannot be moved simply because we possess the strength to push against it.

There are moments when wisdom asks something different of us.

Look again.

There may be another opening.

Another route.

Another way of reaching what matters without abandoning the purpose of the journey.

To change direction is not necessarily to surrender.

A Chief must understand this.

An Elder must understand it as well.

Leadership is not proving that one's first direction was correct.

Leadership is remaining faithful to the responsibility, even when wisdom tells us the route must change.

The Fox does not teach me how to adapt.

Life has required that of me many times.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Fox simply reminds me that sometimes wisdom is recognizing another way.

THE LYNX

Observation • Solitude • Inner Knowing • Recognition

Expanded Teaching

The Lynx reminds me that there are things we recognize only when the noise around us becomes quiet.

I have learned that solitude is not always loneliness.

Sometimes we must step away from the voices surrounding us long enough to hear the questions within ourselves.

There are things we already know but have not yet found words for.

There are memories waiting beneath other memories.

There are truths we have carried for years before understanding why they remained with us.

The quiet does not create those truths.

It gives them room to become visible.

As an Elder, I understand more and more that reflection is also part of responsibility.

Before I can tell another person what I have learned, I must first be willing to sit quietly with what life has taught me.

The Lynx does not give me inner knowledge.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Lynx reminds me to create enough quiet to recognize what may already be there.

THE OTTER

Movement • Renewal • Adaptability • Life

Expanded Teaching

The Otter reminds me that life must also contain movement, renewal, and the ability to breathe again.

Not every lesson arrives through suffering.

That is important to remember.

There are times when the water that carries memory also carries us away from what has become too heavy.

We can move with it.

We can surface.

We can breathe.

We can discover that after everything life has asked us to survive, we are still permitted to experience curiosity, beauty, laughter, companionship, and wonder.

Perhaps this too is part of survival.

Because if those who tried to destroy something succeeded in taking away every possibility of joy from those who survived, part of that destruction would still be continuing.

Renewal therefore becomes its own quiet declaration:

I am still here, and I am still capable of living.

The Otter does not create that capacity within me.

Life has already shown me that sorrow and joy can occupy the same journey.

What We Are Asked to Remember

The Otter simply reminds me that surviving is not the end of the lesson. We must remember how to live.
